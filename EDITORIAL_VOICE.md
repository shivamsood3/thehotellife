# The Hotel Life editorial voice

All signed reviews, guides, features and hotel lists should read as the named
writer's first-person account or judgment. Use **I**, **me** and **my** for an
individual byline. Reserve **we** for an explicitly collective editorial piece.
Do not describe the writer from outside the story (for example, "Zinnia found"
or "on Shivam's stay").

First person is a point of view, not a licence to invent experience. A writer
may say what they observed during a confirmed stay and may explain what they
would book or avoid. Do not turn information sourced from a hotel website,
another publication or a booking page into a claimed personal observation.
When first-hand notes are missing, obtain them from the writer before adding
sensory detail, service encounters or an "I stayed" claim. Keep the useful
reported facts and any relevant caveats in the meantime.

The voice should remain natural: not every sentence needs a pronoun, but the
piece should have an identifiable narrator throughout. Avoid repeating the
same first-person formula across properties. Before publication, read the full
article aloud for unexplained switches between **I**, **we**, impersonal advice
and third-person references to its own author.
